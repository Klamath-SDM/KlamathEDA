KFNFH Historical Collections and Releases Summary (2016–FY24)

  • Larvae collected: The total number of wild larval suckers collected, primarily from the lower Williamson River, for production purposes
  • SARP release: The number of “Production Fish” released. These are fish raised to the target size of approximately 200 mm total length (TL), typically taking 18–24 months
  • Fingerling release: The number of smaller, excess fish stocks (beyond rearing capacity) that were transferred or released into off-channel rearing locations
  • Fry release: The number of small fish released, generally produced from experimental or wild-spawning activities on the lake and river side, or transferred to other facilities
  • Salvage release: The number of suckers salvaged during the year (e.g., from Klamath Project Operations or dam removal efforts) that were rehabilitated, tagged, and then repatriated (released back to the wild)
  • TL vs SL: The average Standard Length (SL) or Total Length (TL) are both in millimeters for the salvaged fish, however, they are inconsistently collected- SL in earlier years (2018–2021) and TL in later years (2023–2024)

Wild Adult LRS Handled

Inventory of the individual wild adult Lost River Suckers (LRS) handled by KFNFH staff during the spawning collection efforts at the East Side Springs (ESS) of Upper Klamath Lake in 2024. The table summarizes the wild LRS captured, identifying their physical characteristics, tagging information, and how their gametes were used.

  • Lists the four dates when gamete collection occurred: April 17, April 24, May 1, and May 8, 2024.
  • documents the total of 43 wild adult LRS handled (13 females and 30 males). All these fish were returned safely to the water thereafter.
  • confirms the sex of the fish and whether they were “Spawned” or “Not Spawned”. Twenty-four of the 30 males were spawned.
  • gamete_use column notes they were “Cryo Preservation” or “Spawned/Cryopreservation,” indicating that excess milt was used for cryopreservation trials.
  • family_groups: most females were crossed with four males, though one female was crossed with two, totaling 50 family groups collected overall. This column applies only to the females.

Pond Monitoring

Pond, collection year, stocking date, number of suckers on stocking date, average weight per fish, total weight of all fish in each pond (used to derive feeding amounts), average total length on stocking date (mm, measured or derived from length weight relationships, date harvested, number of suckers on harvest date, growth, and survival for ponds during the production cycle starting in the Spring of 2023 through the Fall of 2023 at the Klamath Falls National Fish Hatchery.

  • P-series vs A-series ponds: two sets of outdoor rearing spaces distinguished primarly by size and operational status
  • P-Series pond: Small ponds, typically 0.03-acre each,
    • Primary growout space.
    • Twenty-two P-series ponds remained operational throughout most of FY24
  • A-series ponds: Larger ponds, typically 0.25-acre each
    • Demolished early in FY24
  • “start_weight_fish” (Average Weight Per Fish in Grams): This column records the average weight of a single sucker within a given pond lot at the time the fish were initially stocked into that pond
  • “start_wt_g” (Total Weight of All Fish in Grams): This column records the cumulative total weight (biomass) of all the suckers in that specific pond lot at the time they were stocked (“Start Date”). This total weight is the critical metric used by staff to derive daily feeding amounts for the fish in that pond
